Amir Carmel

Postdoctoral researcher, Weizmann Institute of Science

amir.carmel@weizmann.ac.il CV DBLP Google Scholar

Modern datasets are often too large to read in full, let alone process repeatedly. This raises a natural question: can we solve optimization problems while looking at only a small part of the data? Much of my recent work studies this question. When does a small subset of the input, computed cheaply and ideally by uniform sampling, preserve near-optimal solutions for the entire dataset, and how small can it be? This leads naturally to sublinear and streaming algorithms, coresets, and metric embeddings.

More broadly, I am interested in theoretical computer science, and I also work on combinatorial algorithms and string algorithms.

I completed my MSc in mathematics under the supervision of Rom Pinchasi at the Technion, and my PhD and MSc in computer science at Ben-Gurion University, under the supervision of Michal Ziv-Ukelson and Dekel Tsur.

I am currently a postdoc at the Weizmann Institute of Science, hosted by Robert Krauthgamer. Before that, I was a postdoc at Penn State, hosted by Debarati Das.
